# AI document-review workflow: edge cases and recovery

Investigated November 7, 2026 against commit `67544c2` on `codex/ai-workflow-integration`. This document interprets [AI_WORKFLOW_REQUIREMENTS.md](AI_WORKFLOW_REQUIREMENTS.md), which remains the behavioral specification. It records source-level findings, not production certification or authorization to change infrastructure. It is a practical risk inventory, not an exhaustive list of every possible failure.

The implementation has durable jobs, scoped fingerprints, validated CSV citations, fenced commits and pending assessment coalescing. It does **not** yet have durable upload batches, a bounded batch barrier, general invalidation reconciliation, reusable extraction artifacts, or the specified criterion-derived fulfillment contract. Those distinctions matter: rejecting an obsolete response prevents a wrong write, but does not ensure a replacement check ever runs.

## How to read the matrix

- **Implemented** means the narrow behavior described is present in inspected code. It does not mean every race, deployment or user journey has been exercised.
- **Partial** means safeguards exist but an important part of the required behavior or recovery is absent.
- **Missing** means the requested workflow is not present; underlying schema or generic job infrastructure alone does not count as implementation.
- “Required behavior” and “User status and recovery” describe the target. The final column distinguishes what users actually have today. Proposed status wording is illustrative, not an existing API enum.
- P0 tests protect authorization, evidence integrity and truthful results; P1 tests protect workflow completion and bounded recovery; P2 tests address operational resilience and usability. A missing behavior remains an unmet acceptance test, not an expected passing test.

Current limits are deliberately narrower than upload acceptance. Uploads allow PDF, XLSX, CSV, JPG/JPEG, PNG and WebP up to 25 MB. Images are decoded with a 40-million-pixel bound; XLSX validation bounds declared expanded bytes to 100 MiB and entries to 10,000. AI preparation supports only UTF-8 comma-separated CSV: at most 250,000 bytes, 501 logical records including the header, 20 columns and 2,000 characters per cell. A task allows at most 10 documents, 50 candidate requirements and 20 criteria per requirement; serialized evidence has an 80,000-byte cap. The executor additionally compares total prompt/instructions/schema UTF-8 bytes plus framing against its configured input-token allowance (24,000 by default), so the effective limit can be lower. These are rejection bounds, not guarantees of semantic quality or throughput.

## Critical invariants

1. **Saved is not reviewed.** Upload persistence, preparation, matching, assessment, freshness and lender decision are separate facts. A saved file or completed match cannot establish requirement fulfillment.
2. **A result names its exact scope.** Immutable document IDs and hashes, relevant definitions, relationship/evidence identity, analysis/model version, initiating user and borrower/lender scope determine reuse. Lender results additionally bind to a submission. A cache hit cannot bypass authorization.
3. **No stale publication.** Commit rechecks membership, authority, consent epoch, lease token, deadline and current fingerprint under the mutation lock. An old worker cannot publish after recovery, cancellation or a conflicting change.
4. **Invalidation must converge.** Affected results become visibly outdated and authorized latest-state work is eventually scheduled, even if no new upload or manual button press occurs. Current freshness gates implement the first safety property more fully than the second liveness property.
5. **Human intent wins.** AI cannot remove human links, undo explicit human rejections or accept/waive requirements. An explicit human relink may clear a prior rejection. Corrections retain provenance.
6. **Coverage cannot disappear silently.** Every supplied match candidate and criterion must be represented. Unsupported files, failed members, omitted evidence, ambiguity and contradictions cannot produce a fully fulfilled current result.
7. **No evidence laundering.** Exact citations must refer to authorized immutable source versions. Schema-valid text or a real quoted cell does not itself prove that a conclusion is true. Content and requirement text are untrusted data.
8. **No cross-scope leakage.** Draft files, relationships and private notes must not reach a lender through prompts, results, downloads, status, diagnostics or errors. Operator diagnostic access is a separate explicit trust boundary.
9. **Bounded work with durable history.** One pending latest assessment successor per application/user/scope/requirement; running inputs stay immutable. Recovery is bounded, writes are idempotent, and repeated delivery cannot multiply domain effects.
10. **Uncertain execution is not free execution.** Reserve before dispatch; retain uncertain charges after timeouts/crashes. Exactly-once provider billing cannot be guaranteed by exactly-once domain effects.
11. **Failures remain recoverable without AI.** Saved evidence and manual review remain available during an AI outage. Never convert transport, integrity or authorization failures into “not fulfilled.”
12. **Rollback preserves evidence.** Source rollback, database rollback, private-file restoration and external-provider execution are different operations. Preserve history and coordinate compatible versions; a Git tag cannot restore private evidence.

## Recommended implementation order and unresolved policy choices

First preserve existing freshness, consent, idempotency and submitted-scope tests. Then implement the durable batch lifecycle/barrier together with failure-path affected-requirement tracking, so a failed match cannot strand human-linked evidence. Next add durable invalidation reconciliation for definitions, relationships, new requirements and eventual replacement/removal paths. Design criterion rules and derived status before exposing the specified fulfillment vocabulary. Reusable extraction and wider formats require independent quality fixtures and coverage accounting.

Decisions still needed include the measured barrier timeout; whether/how users opt into refreshing old files; treatment of an explicit rejection of an unlinked suggestion; relationship inheritance on replacement; mandatory/optional/alternative criterion semantics; physical deletion and historical evidence retention; operator diagnostic access and idle pruning ownership; and backlog/cost limits suitable for measured demand. These should be resolved in the relevant implementation increment rather than guessed by a worker or model.
